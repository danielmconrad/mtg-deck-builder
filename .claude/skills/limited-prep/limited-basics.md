# Limited fundamentals

These hold across sets. A set's own guide overrides them where it gives different numbers. Facts from a source link it; the rest is common Limited practice. The store's rules win on the night.

## How the events run

- **Prerelease (Sealed):** the week before a set's release, at a local store. Each player gets a Prerelease Pack. For recent sets that's six Play Boosters, one traditional foil promo, a deck box and a Spindown die ([Wizards, Star Trek Prerelease Guide](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/star-trek-prerelease-guide)). You build a deck of at least 40 cards from the pack plus any number of basic lands, and the store supplies the basics. Cards you don't play are your sideboard. Then you play rounds of one-on-one games against other attendees. An evening usually runs four to five hours including the build ([senseos.gg](https://senseos.gg/guides/magic-the-gathering/prerelease-explained/)). The number of rounds, best-of-one or best-of-three, and prizes vary by store, so ask. Play is "casual, friendly," and nothing has to be brought.
- **Booster Draft:** two to eight players, usually eight, each with three boosters. Everyone opens a pack, takes one card without showing it, and passes the rest. Pack 1 goes left, pack 2 right and pack 3 left, until every card is taken. Then each player builds a deck of 40 or more from their picks plus basics ([Wizards, Booster Draft](https://magic.wizards.com/en/game-info/gameplay/formats/booster-draft)). Play Boosters hold 14 cards, so a pool is about 42 cards.
- **Bring:** optionally sleeves, a playmat, dice or counters, tokens and a pen for life totals. Nothing is required.

## Building in Sealed

1. **Sort the pool** by color, with multicolor, colorless and lands in their own piles.
2. **Find the two deepest colors.** Count playables and removal per color, then look for the bombs. Two colors is the default. A light third-color splash is fine for a bomb or premium removal if the pool has fixing (dual lands, mana rocks, fetch effects).
3. **Check the signposts:** a gold uncommon in your pair says what the pair wants, but build from what the pool actually holds.
4. **Removal first:** play nearly all of it in your colors. Wizards suggests at least five removal spells.
5. **Threats:** two or three cards in your colors that win if unanswered.
6. **Curve the creatures.** Sealed is "all about creatures," so 15 to 17 is typical. Wizards' average curve for 23 spells: one-drops 1–2, two-drops 7–8, three-drops 5–6, four-drops 3–4, five-drops 2–3, six or more 0–1.
7. **17 lands** with 23 spells, split by how many cards of each color you play and how early you need each color. Go to 16 for a low curve, or 18 for a top-heavy deck or one that needs lands on the battlefield.
8. **Cut** situational cards, cards that need a third color, and anything you'd be unhappy to draw.
9. **Sideboard:** remember what's in it, such as artifact or flying hate, or a faster or slower build, and ask whether the event allows sideboarding between games.

## Building after a Draft

The same template, but the pool is smaller and was picked for a plan. Play your two colors, and keep anything you took speculatively in the sideboard.

## Draft picks

- **Pick order (BREAD):** **B**ombs, **R**emoval, **E**vasive creatures, **A**ggressive or efficient creatures, **D**ud filler last. Early in pack 1, take the most powerful card. Later, take the best card in your colors.
- **Stay open** for the first six or so picks, then settle into the colors that keep flowing. Strong cards in a color arriving late in pack 1 mean the players to your right aren't taking it, so that color is open. Pack 2 comes from the other direction, so signals there are weaker, and pack 3 comes from the same neighbors as pack 1.
- **Signposts and archetype payoffs** are worth more once you're in that pair. Early on they're just two-color cards.
- **Count playables** near the end of pack 2. You need about 23, with enough two- and three-drops.

## Playing

- **Read open mana:** before attacking into untapped lands, think about what the opponent's colors can do at instant speed for that much mana. The set's tricks list is on the cheat sheet.
- **Mulligans:** a seven-card hand with two lands, or with five or more, is usually a mulligan in a 17-land deck.
- **Ask a judge or store staff** when a rule is unclear. At a Prerelease that's expected, not a penalty.
